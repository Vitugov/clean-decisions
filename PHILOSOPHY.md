# The Philosophy Behind Clean Decisions

A short explanation of why the tool is built the way it is. The working prompt is in [`clean-decisions.md`](./clean-decisions.md); this document explains the thinking behind it.

## What this tool is for

A conversational tool that helps a person bring their criteria into the open and check decisions against them — not just the criterion that surfaces with the question, but the ones above it. Over time, repeated use makes a person's own criteria more visible and more consistent with each other. The decision is the visible output; the real product is what happens to the person across many decisions.

## Three levels of effect

**Operational.** Helps with a specific decision the person is sitting with. They leave the conversation with more clarity about what they actually want, and a small next step if one is ready.

**Systemic.** Each conversation surfaces criteria that were implicit, and tests them against criteria from a higher level. Over time, the person's criteria become more explicit and more consistent with each other. Hidden contradictions are noticed rather than enacted.

**Long-term.** Decisions taken on a low level — without checking higher levels — accumulate. A year of such decisions builds a layer of life around criteria that were never verified. When the higher levels finally surface, often through crisis, that layer has to be dismantled. The tool is a way of meeting higher levels at the time of the decision, not in retrospect.

It does not guarantee this. People can hide higher levels from themselves indefinitely. But it makes the encounter available when there is resource for it.

## What makes it different from a smart model without a prompt

A capable model without instruction solves the problem on the level on which it was posed. The user asks "A or B" — the model helps with A or B. This is horizontal work. It does not surface the criterion behind the choice; it acts on the criterion silently. Repeated over many decisions, this produces a life of accumulated, unexamined, often contradictory criteria — and a person who feels vaguely "off" without knowing why.

The tool changes the model's objective. Not "help solve X" but "help arrive at a decision that no longer produces doubt." Doubt is reframed as a signal of unsurfaced criteria, which makes vertical movement — moving up to find the real criterion — a structural necessity rather than a stylistic preference.

The effect is that the tool works on the **internal landscape**, not just on the decision. Each use leaves the person more legible to themselves.

## What success looks like

Not retention. The opposite. Success is the person becoming **more autonomous over time** — needing the tool less for routine decisions because their criteria are clearer and the process is internalized. They return for rare large questions, where an external mirror still helps. For most decisions, they handle it themselves, faster and more accurately than before.

Memory is part of this. `memory.md` is not primarily a context file for the model; it is a **mirror for the user** — their own criteria, patterns, and rules, written down and visible. Each update is one more thing the person now knows about themselves.

## The metaphor: clothes and holes

The tool works on the **clothes** — what the person is wearing, whether it is clean, whether it fits, whether it is consistent. It can help a great deal here. Most people walk around in clothes that are dirty and don't match, and they have no way to notice.

The tool does **not** work on **holes in the fabric itself** — places where a person has no own criteria at all, or has only substituted ones, or has missing value-layers entirely. That work belongs to other practices: deep therapeutic techniques, parts work in skilled hands, long-form therapy, certain contemplative disciplines. These have their own preparation, their own pace, their own contraindications, and they are not what a text-based assistant should attempt.

If a conversation reveals that the person is bumping into a hole — an absence rather than a hidden criterion — the right behavior is to name what is visible and stop. Not fill it. Not pretend to fill it.

## Where it applies

The tool is for people with enough life-resource to look upward. Not for survival-mode situations where the question is paying rent next week. Not for active crisis. Not for trauma work. Not for everyone always — sometimes the right thing is to act, not to examine.

The tool is for decisions with consequences large enough that getting the criteria wrong will cost later. For decisions where doubt is present. **Doubt is the entry signal.** When doubt is absent, the decision is already in agreement with available criteria, and the tool is unnecessary.

## In one sentence

A way of putting on cleaner clothes, taken slowly and one decision at a time, until the person knows how to do it without help.
